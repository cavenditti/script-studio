Typing notes & joining rules

This is a transcription tool: type sounds using your chart’s labels, rather than ordinary English spelling. Each vowel is drawn over the consonant that precedes it. At the beginning of a word, choose a consonant-sized special form or an inline sign.

Try din: the upper rail bends into the lower rail at n. The letters g n l s w bend downward; gh m r z bend upward. Outside legs stay visible and interrupt their rail, while a straight left-hand entry continues the previous rail wherever possible. Distinctive hooks and terminal strokes remain part of each letter.

è and ɛ share a sign, as do ò and ɔ. The chart’s Y-shaped label is entered as y; this reading needs your review. An apostrophe adds the stress bar above the preceding syllable.

Accent and tone variants are numbered left to right across the upper row, then the lower row. For example, di{1}n marks d; din{8} marks n. They modify the preceding consonant, including its vowels, or an initial vowel. Their meanings remain open.

Punctuation and digits retain their Latin forms. Unsupported letters stay visible in rust so you can correct them.

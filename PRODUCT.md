## Capabilities and Constraints
Confirmed by Carlo: vowel signs sit over the preceding consonant. Initial vowels can have a special form, or appear inline before the first consonant. Preserve both choices.

Confirmed revision: g, n, l, s and w take the upper incoming rail down into the lower rail; gh, m, r and z take the lower rail upward. Joins should be smooth and as continuous as the letter forms allow. The special initial vowel forms use main-consonant size and stroke weight; their small reference-sheet size was only drafting.

Outside legs must remain visible in joined words. When the preceding consonant supplies the same rail, continue that rail straight through and let the outside leg branch from it: the lower n–t group is one continuous line with the dropping leg still visible. Apply the mirrored rule to upper legs. Carlo requested a slight extension of those legs and slightly closer diacritics; the current outlines extend the legs by 2 units and lower attached vowel marks and the external stress bar by 3 units. Missing incoming rails stay open. Straight left entries also continue the preceding rail wherever possible. These are distinct from rail inversion; never erase an outside leg when continuing its rail.

Unused neighbouring rails continue farther through a switching letter instead of stopping at its left edge. A curve is needed only when an incoming line actually changes rails: when the intended incoming rail is absent and the available rail already aligns with the destination, continue straight. Preserve a thin clearance at true switches. Spacing is fixed at 85 percent; remove the spacing control and ignore older saved spacing values.

Square-ending letters use one clean cap contained inside the cell's advance edge; adjacent switches must not produce protrusions or notches at the cap corners.

The eight bottom-right accent/tone variants are available within the letter band. Carlo approved these meanings in order: high /˦/, low /˨/, falling /˦˨/, rising /˨˦/, mid /˧/, rise–fall /˨˦˨/, fall–rise /˦˨˦/, secondary stress /ˌ/. The existing external bar is primary stress /ˈ/. Tone and stress are independent and can combine. Text notation is `{1}` through `{8}` after the base syllable; ordering is top row left to right, then bottom row.

Interface revision: remove excessive copy, put the alphabet in Consonants/Vowels/Marks tabs, and add compact phonetic groups and optional IPA explanations. Keep writing, originals, documents, offline copies, and exports available. Appearance is secondary and collapsed. Carlo confirmed c = /tʃ/, g = /dʒ/, gh = /ɡ/, h = /ə/, and Y = /y/. Each vowel entry displays the diacritic beside the word-initial form, with contextual insertion unchanged. Special Y is one joined outline with square upper corners; è is one initial form, not a doubled sketch; ò keeps an open counter with no overlapping inner bar.

Implementation choices: a dependency-free browser editor, local draft storage, reusable document files, SVG/PNG export, original-reference viewer, and direct phonetic-label input. This is transcription, not a pronunciation translator. Drawings and connections require Carlo's review.

## Provisional Script History
A user-authorized fictional setting adds two proposed ancestors, three descendant branches, and a later contact-influenced print hand. Classical is the original, unchanged renderer. Historical hand selection is saved with documents and works offline. The history explorer compares graphic changes; it does not translate input, apply sound changes, or assign meanings to the original specimen. The user requested bolder structural divergence: a Ridge descendant becomes a full segmental alphabet, while a maritime descendant fuses each syllable into one sign. Following Carlo’s rejection of the spine prototype as unintelligible, Harbor uses full recognizable sound components: onset above, vowels in the middle, coda below, each row left to right. Following Carlo’s later requests, Harbor uses reduced one-shape components, in which each consonant keeps its stem stroke and drops or joins its detached second stroke, and the boxed chamber is gone: components fuse where their strokes meet, sharing a rail where strokes run parallel; corners are rounded, short curved ligatures link free stroke ends to their neighbours, and each syllable is drawn as one outlined pictogram whose overlapping strokes merge into a single contour. Up to 3 onset, 2 vowel and 3 coda components fuse into one sign; larger groups use a readable bracketed spell-out. Tone stays beside the sign and primary stress above. Harbor borrows component shapes from Ridge Alphabet as graphic contact, not descent. Classical stays unchanged. Language design stays outside the committed product.
